POP 1 · Formal Observation

Westward Expansion & Manifest Destiny

Feb 5, 2026 · Rudder Middle School · 8th Grade U.S. History · 45 min · 25 students

Lesson Plan & SAC
TEKS: 8.6(B), 8.10(A)(B) ELPS: 2C, 2G, 3D, 3E, 3H, 4D

What this lesson covered

45-minute 8th-grade U.S. History lesson on territorial expansion. Students identified major U.S. land acquisitions (1783–1853) and analyzed how geographic, economic, and political factors drove Manifest Destiny.

  • Central focus: Westward growth, Louisiana Purchase, and Manifest Destiny (TEKS 8.6(B), 8.10(A)(B)).
  • Hook: Manifest Destiny fill-in-the-blank Do Now analyzing John O'Sullivan's concept.
  • Core task: Interactive journal mapping organizing the 7 territories in chronological order using the mnemonic ULFTOMG ("You Left, OMG!").
  • Exit Ticket: 4-column categorization sorting territories by acquisition method (War/Treaty, Bought, Annex, Ceded).

45-minute Civil War review before a unit assessment. Students analyzed causes, major events, and key individuals, then explained reasoning with grade-level vocabulary.

POP 3 · Formal Observation

Freak the Mighty: Symbolism & Coat of Arms

Sep 16, 2026 · Jefferson Middle School · 6th Grade ELAR · 60 min · 30 students

Lesson Plan & SAC
TEKS: 6.7(B), 6.6(E), 6.6(H) ELPS: 3E, 3H, 4F, 5F, 5G 60 Minutes

What this lesson covered

Students defined heraldic symbolism and explained how authors use knights, armor, and crests to represent character identity in Freak the Mighty, then designed a personal four-quadrant Coat of Arms with a short written analysis.

1. Hook: "Kevin's Arthur Clues" linking braces to humanity's first robot suits.
2. Direct Instruction: Read-aloud Chapters 7–9; chivalric code principles.
3. Pop Culture Bridge: Anime/gaming squad insignia (Asta / *Black Clover*).
4. Application: 4-quadrant Coat of Arms & 3-sentence written analysis.

Classroom Management

Expectations, redirection sequence, and differentiation

Classroom Management Plan

"I don't talk over students. I wait for their attention, keep the routines the same every day, and give students the same respect I expect from them."

1. Focus on your own choices.
2. Report only serious concerns to an adult.
3. "Mute people with your mind." Tune out distractions and stay out of trouble.
4. Active speaker respect: Stay seated and quiet while instruction occurs.

Off-Task Redirection Sequence

Step 1 Name & Eye Contact
Step 2 Redirect & Move Seat
Step 3 Student Conference & Parent Contact

Targeted Differentiation

"I use sentence stems so every student has a way into writing and discussion, and I seat students on purpose so each one can focus and get support. Through Tier 2 dyslexia intervention and small-group instruction, I give students with IEPs and dyslexia the targeted support they need."

Sentence Stems & Frames: Multi-tiered written stems providing accessible entry points for Emergent Bilinguals and SPED learners.
Strategic Seating: Purposeful placement aligning peer helpers, proximity management, and low-distraction zones.
Small-Group Intervention: Targeted Tier 2 phonics, fluency, and comprehension reteaching during independent practice cycles.
Daily Entry Routine: 1. Binder & Pencil → 2. Seat → 3. Warm-up → 4. Lesson
